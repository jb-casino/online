Understanding wagering requirements

This section matters more than any bonus percentage, so it's worth slowing down.

A wagering requirement (sometimes shown as "WR" or "playthrough") is the number of times you must bet a bonus before you can withdraw anything won from it. If a bonus of a given amount carries a 35x requirement, you must place total bets equal to 35 times that bonus amount before the bonus and its winnings become withdrawable.

Points to check in any casino's terms, JB included:

  • The multiplier — how many times the bonus (or bonus + deposit) must be wagered.
  • What counts — slots usually contribute 100%, while table games and live casino often contribute far less or nothing.
  • Maximum bet during wagering — exceeding a per-spin cap can void the bonus.
  • Time limit — bonuses commonly expire within a set number of days.
  • Maximum cash-out — free-spin winnings are sometimes capped.

None of this makes a bonus "bad" — it makes it a structured offer. The mistake is treating the headline number as free cash. Read the playthrough first.

Is online gambling legal in India?

This is the part every India-based reader should take seriously, because there is no single national answer.

  • Gambling law in India is largely decided at the state level, so the rules where you live may differ sharply from another state.
  • Some states permit certain forms of online gaming; others restrict or prohibit it, and a few have moved specifically against online real-money games.
  • The distinction between "games of skill" and "games of chance" is legally significant in India and is still being contested and clarified.
  • Offshore operators licensed abroad (such as in Curaçao) are not licensed by any Indian authority, which affects what recourse you have if something goes wrong.

The responsible step is straightforward: check the current law in your own state before playing, and understand that using an offshore site does not place you under Indian regulatory protection.
